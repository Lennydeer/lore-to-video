# Research and local assets

## Establish the interval

Record the title, adaptation/version, exact opening and closing events, spoiler policy, and existing project location. Ask only for missing choices that affect the work. Keep the user's narrower scope; do not turn a rendering test into fresh research of the entire franchise.

## Separate evidence from reconstruction

Prefer the original work and official episode guides, scripts, maps, artbooks, and credits. Use secondary sources to find leads, then check consequential facts where possible. Record URLs and episode/chapter/timestamp references. Mark conflicting accounts, unknown dates, and inferred routes explicitly. Do not blend different adaptations without the user's instruction.

Keep world facts in `lore.md`, location descriptions in `geography.md`, and actions in `chronology.md`. Each event needs its boundaries, in-world date or uncertainty, place, participants and sides, action, outcome, and supporting references. A location introduction is not an event.

For each character, check period, appearance, clothing, identity, and transformations. Determine faction membership and temporary cooperation at the time of the event, not from a general character summary.

## Collect actual files

Save original map images, portraits, unit emblems, and approved music locally. An image-search label alone does not establish identity: open the image and inspect it. Preserve originals separately from crops or other prepared inputs.

Maintain `assets.csv` with asset ID, local path, source URL, source location, character/form/period where relevant, official or reconstruction status, and verification notes. Verify that the file opens and matches the intended role. When copying an existing project's assets, preserve its provenance and identify the source project.

For maps, record whether distances and positions are canonical, reconstructed, or schematic. Do not make an animation appear geographically exact when the underlying evidence is approximate.

For music, maintain `soundtrack.md` with track, performer/composer, source, local-file status, selected passage, and credits. Use the approved recording; do not silently choose a cover, remix, or replacement. A streaming link does not supply an audio file or establish permission to use the recording. Report missing audio and ask for the user's chosen way forward; a silent sample requires agreement.

## Handoff to design

A useful project dossier contains:

```text
README.md                scope, current stage, decisions, gaps
lore.md                  world facts
geography.md             locations and map status
chronology.md            dated actions and outcomes
sources.md               evidence and citations
assets.csv               local asset inventory
soundtrack.md            selected recording and credits
maps/
characters/images/
factions/emblems/
music/
```

Adapt an existing structure rather than reorganizing it needlessly. Check coverage of the requested endpoint, event-specific participants, asset identity, source traceability, and music availability before describing the dossier as ready.
